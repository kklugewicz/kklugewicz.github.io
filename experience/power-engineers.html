Overview

At POWER Engineers I designed real distribution circuits for Oncor projects, working to cut cost and raise reliability while meeting TxDOT and client requirements. Each project ended in a construction package a field crew could build from.

How a project ran

  • Scope. I coordinated directly with Oncor project managers to align on scope, secure approvals, and resolve field constraints.
  • Design. I laid out the overhead or underground circuit to fit the site, the client's standards, and the budget.
  • Calculations. I ran the engineering calculations that prove the design works: conductor sag, guy-wire loading, cable pull, and cost.
  • Package. I produced the full construction package and checked the drawings with drafters before submission.
  • Tracking. I tracked milestones, risks, and deliverables for every project in Smartsheet.

Design types

LayoutWhat it is
Double-circuitTwo circuits carried on one line of poles, which saves right-of-way and structures.
ArmlessConductors mounted on insulators attached directly to the pole, with no crossarms, for a narrower and cleaner profile.
Underground residential (URD)Buried cable feeding residential areas, with pull calculations to make sure cable can be installed through the conduit.

Engineering calculations

  • Conductor sag: how far a span droops between poles, so it keeps the required clearance over roads and ground.
  • Guy-wire loading: the tension guy wires must carry to keep poles stable where lines turn or end.
  • Cable pull: the tension on underground cable during installation, so it stays within the cable's limits.

Designs near state roads also had to satisfy Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) rules.

Deliverables

Every project ended with a complete construction package: permits, a bill of materials, and field-ready construction prints.
