Lithium Battery Cycler

A 120 V AC bench instrument that charges and discharges lithium cells under user-defined profiles while tracking capacity and cell health.

Overview

The cycler runs off wall power and lets a user set constant-current and constant-voltage charge and discharge profiles from an on-device display. It reports C-rate, state of health, depth of discharge, and coulombic efficiency. We met every target specification.

Central PCB (my ownership)

  • Selected and justified the core parts: an ESP32-DEVKITC-32UE, MPM3610 buck converters (21 V → 3.3 V / 5 V), and 16 A-rated relays.
  • Built the full GPIO map, integrated every subsystem into one schematic, and laid out the board in EasyEDA with a dedicated ground plane.
  • Diagnosed four bring-up defects: an unbonded battery ground, an I²C level-shifter bus conflict, relay traces undersized for 5 A, and a connector mismatch between boards. I fixed each on the bench.
  • Recovered a board after desoldering damage by moving its components to a replacement PCB.

Sensing

  • Voltage sense uses an INA333 instrumentation amplifier. Current sense uses a Hall-effect sensor, characterized at 12.3 mV/A.
  • Both sensors were checked against a multimeter across their ranges. Instrumentation-amp error was 0.5% at 4.0 V.
  • Built a noise-rejection chain: a relay-open baseline, a trimmed mean of the middle 30% of samples, and an exponential moving average.

Charge & discharge

  • Charging uses an LTM8026 CC/CV module, simulated in LTspice and driven by an MCP4725 DAC.
  • Discharging uses a MOSFET active load (IRF540N) with an LT1006 integrator loop. A FLIR thermal study set a 4 A safe ceiling per MOSFET, and we derated the design to 3 A.
  • Firmware supports CC, CV, constant-resistance, and constant-power modes, a full-cycle mode, and an emergency stop.

The design followed IEC 62133, UL 2054, UL 62368-1, and OSHA lab-safety practice.
