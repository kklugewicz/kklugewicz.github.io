Dual-Band Patch Antenna

A U-slot microstrip patch antenna designed for 2.45 and 5 GHz, simulated in Ansys HFSS, milled on FR4, and measured on a VNA.

Overview

A dual-band patch resonates at two frequencies from a single radiator. Here the outer patch sets the 2.4 GHz band, and a U-shaped slot cut into it creates the 5 GHz band. That makes it cheap, light, and easy to fabricate.

Design

  • Started from textbook rectangular-patch theory (half-wave resonator, effective permittivity, fringing correction) to size the patch for the lower band.
  • Modeled the antenna in HFSS with a 50 Ω lumped port, swept 1–6 GHz, and tuned the slot and feed, since the U-slot adds coupling the closed-form equations miss.
  • Checked S11, directivity, and surface-current distributions at both bands.

Build & test

  • Milled the design as a copper microstrip on FR4 with an edge-mount coax connector.
  • Measured on a VNA, with resonances at 2.53 GHz and 4.94 GHz. Cleaning the board and reseating the connector noticeably improved the measurements.

What I learned

The patch dimensions control the primary band and the slot controls the secondary one, so the narrow bandwidth leaves very tight tolerances. Next steps would be a PIN diode across the slot bridge to switch the upper band on and off, and arraying the element for beamforming.
