Edge ECoG Finger-Flexion Decoder

A causal, quantized decoder that reads finger movement from brain signals and runs in real time on a low-cost ARM board.

Overview

Most published ECoG decoders are only ever tested offline, with filters that can see into the future and models too large for an implant-class processor. I wanted to know how well you can decode finger movement when the decoder has to be causal, tiny, and actually running on hardware.

I designed and built the full pipeline myself: preprocessing, model, evaluation protocol, training, scoring, and export. The model is ECoGFlexNet, a ~12,600-parameter temporal convolutional network with channel attention. I trained it on all three subjects of BCI Competition IV Dataset 4 and deployed it to a Radxa Zero 3W single-board computer.

Getting the evaluation right

  • Rebuilt the training and evaluation pipeline to remove data leakage and match the official competition test splits. That fix alone lifted mean correlation from 0.41 to 0.53.
  • Found and corrected four subtle causality defects in the redesign: group delay from causal target smoothing, a look-ahead leak inside the attention block, train and test segments filtered independently, and a staircase artifact from 20 Hz emission.
  • Rejected "best seed" reporting as test-set selection, which would have inflated results by about 0.021. I report a three-seed ensemble as a separate row instead.

Deploying to the edge

  • Quantized to 8-bit integers and exported through ExecuTorch / XNNPACK to an RK3566 (aarch64) board. Correlation held at 0.51.
  • Built an on-device latency harness with four modes (verify, burst, paced, soak). Model median latency is 5.0 ms at about 29.5 MMAC/s.
  • Verified that preprocessing is bit-identical in batch and streaming modes. This is the only work on record for this benchmark that pairs that guarantee with measured deadline-miss statistics.
  • Brought the board up from scratch: flashed Ubuntu, built ExecuTorch for aarch64, resolved a PyTorch ABI mismatch, and traced an SD-card boot failure to the power supply.

Why it matters

The contribution is deployment rigor and efficiency rather than a leaderboard number. A decoder that is honest about causality and proven against real-time deadlines is much closer to something that could run next to a patient.
