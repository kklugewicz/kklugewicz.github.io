Portable EEG Headband

An electrode-to-laptop EEG acquisition system built around the TI ADS1299 biopotential front end and an ESP32.

  • RoleTeam Lead & Firmware
  • WhenFall 2025
  • Team4 engineers
  • CourseEE 3240 · UT Arlington

Overview

Our goal was a low-cost EEG headband that could turn brain-wave activity into control signals for simple software. The system chains a custom electrode headband into a board built around the ADS1299, which amplifies and digitizes microvolt-level signals. An ESP32 then configures that chip and streams the data wirelessly to a laptop for filtering and visualization.

My role

  • Team lead. I coordinated four design areas, ran our meetings, owned the budget and schedule, and submitted every purchase order.
  • Firmware. I wrote the ESP32 firmware that initializes and configures the ADS1299 over SPI, manages acquisition, and streams the digitized samples over Bluetooth.
  • Neuroscience research. I made sure the design followed EEG fundamentals (alpha and beta bands, event-related potentials, electrode placement) and relevant biomedical standards.

Signal chain

Electrodes → ADS1299 (24-bit, programmable gain up to 24×) → ESP32 over SPI → Bluetooth → MATLAB (0.5–35 Hz band-pass and Welch power spectral density).

Validation

  • Verified the converter with the ADS1299's internal test signals at ~1 Hz and ~2 Hz before the PCB arrived.
  • Injected 50 mVpp sine waves at 1.9, 25, and 40 Hz from a signal generator. Each showed a clean peak at the injected frequency in the PSD.
  • Compared bias-drive on and off, and ran dry-electrode and saline-gel contact tests.

Outcome

We finished the headset and a stable acquisition pipeline, from electrode to laptop. Filtering strong enough to pull reliable control features out of live EEG was the piece we didn't finish within the semester. That lesson about real-world noise led directly to my later work on causal decoding.
